Explore the Implications of Climate Change

Goals

Climate change is an ever‑growing global threat that undermines human health and livelihoods. International cooperation efforts have been proposed through agreements such as the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement, which aim to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, limit global temperature rise to well below 2 degrees Celsius (with efforts toward 1.5 degrees), and require countries to regularly strengthen their national climate commitments over time. This project examines the impact of climate change on the United States within a global framework, examining how American climate policy fails to align with the data. The United States has resisted many international climate agreements, positioning itself at odds with the global movement toward cooperative action. The United States signed but never ratified the Kyoto Protocol, making it the only signatory that did not bring the treaty into force domestically, and it has repeatedly moved in and out of full participation in the Paris Agreement. Despite the United States' opposition to global action, the nation faces significant environmental, social, and economic consequences from the impact of climate change.

Key Questions

  • How has the number of climate events changed over time?
  • What are the most common natural disasters?
  • How many lives have been lost to these natural disasters?
  • How much do natural disasters cost?

Data Sources

Global climate data was sourced from EM-DAT, a public international disaster database, and NOAA, the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration Each visualization will explain how the data was processed to create the final product.
