Conclusions

By exploring these visualizations, we have established that climate-related disasters are an increasing threat not only globally, but also to the United States. In the U.S. specifically, tropical cyclones are the deadliest and costliest of all disaster types, despite not being the most prevalent type. However, the Geofluid Fluid Dynamics Laboratory of the NOAA reports that tropical cyclones are worsening as a result of climate warming (3). It is due to anthropogenic activity that tropical cyclone rainfall rates and intensities are increasing. In addition, a larger proportion of tropical cyclones are reaching intense Category 4 and 5 status due to human activity causing our planet to warm.

The United States' political policy does not reflect the increasing urgency of climate change mitigation. On January 20th, 2025, the United States announced its withdrawal from the Paris Agreement, an internationally adopted treaty specificying goals for combating climate change (4). FEMA (Federal Emergency Management Agency) is the organization responsible for coordinating response and recovery efforts after weather disasters, however, they have received significant budget cuts under the Trump administration (5). The NOAA, which provided much of the data for these visualizations, is also facing budget cuts under the Trump administration (6).

We urge Americans to understand the current climate change landscape through our provided visualizations to better inform their voting decisions. We also hope to encourage policymakers to invest in the future of the United States' people and economy by investing in climate change mitigation today.
